# San Diego, Balboa Park

A day wandering Balboa Park in late winter. The architecture here is something else — all Spanish Colonial Revival from the 1915-16 Panama-California Exposition, these incredibly ornate facades that somehow don't feel overdone. Just right for southern California.

## Places in the Park

- Casa del Prado and the Plaza de Panama
- The Botanical Building with its iconic lath structure
- Desert Garden and the succulent collections
- Spreckels Organ Pavilion with its curved colonnade
- El Cid statue in the Plaza de Panama
- Quiet paths between the museums

The light through the Botanical Building's wooden slats is worth the visit alone. Those alternating bars of sun and shadow moving across the ferns and palms all afternoon.

The desert gardens feel very California — all these sculptural succulents and cacti, everything thriving in the sun. Not manicured, just confident.

Down by the Organ Pavilion, that sweeping colonnade and the bamboo gardens feel like a different kind of grandeur. And the El Cid statue — bronze warrior on horseback framed perfectly through one of the arches, California Building tower rising behind. These compositions feel intentional but not forced.

San Diego has this easy relationship with outdoor space. The whole city feels like it's designed to be outside. That carries through to the coast too — the cliffs, the seagulls claiming their posts on the seawalls, the weathered memorial markers facing the Pacific. All of it under that clean, endless blue.
