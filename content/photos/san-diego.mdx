# San Diego, Balboa Park

A day wandering Balboa Park in late summer. The architecture here is something else — all Spanish Colonial Revival from the 1915-16 Panama-California Exposition, these incredibly ornate facades that somehow don't feel overdone. Just right for southern California.

## Places in the Park

- Casa del Prado and the Plaza de Panama
- The Botanical Building with its iconic lath structure
- Desert Garden and the succulent collections
- Spreckels Organ Pavilion and its sweeping colonnade
- El Cid Campeador statue near the museums
- Quiet paths between the museums

The light through the Botanical Building's wooden slats is worth the visit alone. Those alternating bars of sun and shadow moving across the ferns and palms all afternoon.

The desert gardens feel very California — all these sculptural succulents and cacti, everything thriving in the sun. Not manicured, just confident.

The Spreckels Organ Pavilion sits at the southern end of the park — this grand curved colonnade around the world's largest outdoor pipe organ. Even without a concert, the architecture and the way the bamboo shadows fall across the walkway make it worth the detour.

## La Jolla Coast

After the park, I drove over to La Jolla Cove. The contrast is immediate — from manicured gardens to raw Pacific coastline. Seagulls claim the stone seawalls, waves crash against kelp-covered rocks, and old memorial posts stand weathered by salt air.

San Diego has this easy relationship with outdoor space. The whole city feels like it's designed to be outside.
